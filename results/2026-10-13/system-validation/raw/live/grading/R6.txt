## 1. Are career averages taken into account?

**You're mostly right.** Career numbers enter the board in only one place. In the code (`report/wo5/tools/wo5_lines.py`), each line is built like this:

| Category | Source |
|---|---|
| PTS, REB, AST, 3PM, TOV | **2025-26 only**: last season's per-36 rate × projected minutes. No career input. |
| STL, BLK | Last **three** seasons, weighted 5/4/3. No career input. |
| FG%, FT% | 2025-26, pulled **one-third of the way toward career**. This is the only career input. |
| Points | Calculated from the shooting line, not typed in. |

So career averages don't shape counting stats at all. That was a deliberate choice, and the recent-era test supports it. In the table below, lower error is better:

| Method | Error | Beat "last season only" |
|---|---|---|
| Career averages for everything | **0.4176** (worst) | 1 of 7 seasons |
| Last season only | 0.4060 | — |
| **Board's method (as shipped)** | 0.3924 | 7 of 7 |
| Three-season average | 0.3921 | 7 of 7 |
| Half last season, half three-season | **0.3890** (best) | 7 of 7 |

What this means:
- **Pure career averages are the worst predictor tested.** Players change too much: role, age, team. This matches your point that most careers are short and the game has changed.
- **If you mean that more than one season of history should count, the evidence agrees with you.** The half-and-half blend beats the board's method by a small but consistent margin (the 95% range is −0.0053 to −0.0015, clear of zero). The board doesn't use it today.
- **For FG% and FT%, the career pull holds up.** Swapping it for a pull toward three seasons made the board slightly worse (FG% error .2672 vs .2609). One caveat: for long-career veterans, "career" reaches back before 2019, into the era you object to. I didn't test that subgroup separately.

## 2. Are we still following expert projections?

**We check against them, but we don't copy them.** The lines are our own, built from Basketball-Reference totals. Expert projections come in three ways:

1. **Range check.** Our lines are compared with Yahoo (10/6), Hashtag (10/12), RotoBaller (9/29) and each player's 2025-26 actuals. A cell that falls outside all of them is pulled back inside or keeps a written reason. Before the 10/12 rebuild, 131 players were out of range; now 23 players (25 cells) are, each with a reason.
2. **Minutes for players coming back from injury** come straight from the outside projections, because our data can't set them. That covers 10 players, including Haliburton, Davis, Kyrie, Lillard, Trae, Morant and Edey.
3. **Rank comparison.** The board agrees with Rotoworld at 0.828, RotoBaller at 0.824, RotoWire's expert panel at 0.798 and Yahoo's own ranking at 0.839 (1.0 would be identical order).

Where we disagree with the experts, it's on value, not data. We're higher on steals, free-throw and three-point specialists (Daniels, Rollins, Kawhi, Markkanen). We're lower on stars who hurt you in a category, like Giannis' free throws and Trae's turnovers.

**I didn't check for newer expert projections.** The newest file is dated 10/12, and nothing has been pulled since.

## 3. The known gap

Scaling it *up* for players joining thin rosters didn't help, and it made free-throw error 10.5% worse.
