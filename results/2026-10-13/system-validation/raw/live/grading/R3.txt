## 1. You're mostly right: career numbers barely enter the line

The line builder (`report/wo5/tools/wo5_lines.py:2-9`) builds each category like this:

| Category | Source | Career used? |
|---|---|---|
| PTS (via shot volume), REB, AST, 3PM, TOV | **2025-26 only** (per 36 minutes, scaled to projected minutes) | **No** |
| STL, BLK | Last three seasons, weighted 5/4/3 by games | No, three seasons only |
| FG%, FT% | 2025-26 percentage, moved **one-third** of the way toward career | **Yes, at one-third weight** |

The data file runs from 2003-04 to 2025-26, so "career" really is each player's whole career. For a 15-year veteran, that means his FG% and FT% pull reaches back into a different era, which is the same concern you raised about the 20-year test.

So if "career averages are taken into account" was described to you as covering the whole line, that overstates it. Career numbers touch only the two percentages, at one-third weight. Points, rebounds, assists, threes and turnovers come from one season. The backtest confirms this: on those five categories the board's errors are identical to "last season only" (PTS 2.5637, REB 0.8357, AST 0.7106 in both).

## 2. Should career averages count more? The recent-era evidence

All figures are from `scope-fence-2026-10-13/out/modern/modern_history_20mpg.json`. The score is a combined error across the 9 categories, so **lower is better**.

| Method | Combined error | Seasons better than last-season-only |
|---|---|---|
| Career averages only | 0.4176 (worst) | 1 of 7 |
| Last season only | 0.4060 | n/a |
| **The board today (WO-5)** | **0.3924** | **7 of 7** |
| Three seasons, weighted 5/4/3 | 0.3921 | 7 of 7 |
| Half last season, half three seasons | **0.3890 (best)** | 7 of 7 |

What that shows:
- **Pure career averages are the worst method tested.** They beat last-season-only in only 1 of 7 seasons. In this era, a player's past is a weaker guide than his recent seasons.
- **Recent history does help, and the board uses less of it than it could.** The half-and-half method beats the board on every counting category except steals and blocks. Its margin is small but real: 0.0015 to 0.0053 better in 95% of resamples. Its edge is in those five one-season categories.
- **For the two percentages, three seasons beat career.** FG error is 0.2597 with three seasons vs 0.2609 with the board's career pull; FT error is 0.1037 vs 0.1096. So the career pull isn't the best version of the idea even where it's used.

**My assessment:** your objection holds in substance. The board doesn't weight player history the way the description suggests, and the evidence favours more weight on the last two or three seasons, not on full careers. The gain is modest. Nothing here says the current board is badly wrong; it says it could be somewhat sharper.

Two caveats:
- These are backtest errors on past seasons. They don't measure what the change would do to draft ranks, because nobody has computed that.
- I didn't check how the backtest handled minutes. It doesn't change the comparison between methods, but it does mean the absolute error numbers aren't a forecast of this season's accuracy.
