## 1. Where career numbers enter the line

This is from the code that built the board, `report/wo5/tools/wo5_lines.py:6-9` and lines 67–73:

| Category | Source | Career used? |
|---|---|---|
| FGA, FTA, threes, rebounds, assists, turnovers | 2025-26 per-36 rate × projected minutes | **No** |
| **FG%, FT%** | 2025-26 percentage pulled **one-third of the way toward career** | **Yes** |
| Steals, blocks | Three seasons (2025-26/24-25/23-24), weighted 5/4/3 by games | No, three seasons |
| Points | Calculated from the shooting line | Indirectly, through FG%/FT% |

"Career" means every regular season he has on file. Three examples from `wo5/out/lines_wo5_2026-10-12.json`:

- **Giannis:** FG% .624 last season, .554 career, line uses **.600**. FT% .650 / .691 → **.664**. His points drop from 27.6 to 26.9 because of this.
- **Dyson Daniels:** FG% .517 / .488 → **.508**. Steals 1.96 last season become **2.3** on the three-season weighting.
- **Curry:** last season and career are almost the same, so the line barely moves (.468 → .469).

So "career averages aren't being taken into account" is right for counting stats and wrong for shooting percentages.

## 2. Why counting stats don't use career averages

This rests on the **recent-era test only**: target seasons 2019-20 to 2025-26, committed on 10/13 in `scope-fence-2026-10-13/out/modern/`. Your objection to the 20-year window stands, and that run is not used here.

The score below is the average error across the nine categories, scaled so they're comparable. **Lower is better.** Sample: 1,528 player-seasons, players at 20+ minutes the season before.

| Method | All players | Years 2–3 | Years 4–8 | Years 9+ | Seasons better than last-season-only |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Last season only | 0.4060 | 0.4259 | 0.3988 | 0.4061 | — |
| **Current board method** | **0.3924** | 0.4228 | 0.3842 | 0.3885 | **7 of 7** |
| Three-season average | 0.3921 | 0.4238 | 0.3870 | 0.3829 | 7 of 7 |
| Half last season, half three-season | **0.3890** | **0.4210** | **0.3810** | 0.3840 | 7 of 7 |
| **Career averages** | **0.4176** | 0.4245 | 0.4053 | **0.4323** | **1 of 7** |

What the table says:

- **Career averages are the worst method overall.** They beat last-season-only in just 1 of 7 seasons.
- **They're worst of all for veterans (9+ years), which is where they'd matter most.** A 10-year career average still carries a player's early or peak years, which no longer describe him. This is your own era point from 10/12 showing up inside a single career.
- Restricting to starters (26+ minutes) gives the same picture: career 0.4255 vs the board's 0.3921.
- **For percentages, the career pull is better than a shorter window.** FG% error is 0.2609 with the career pull vs 0.2672 when it reaches back only three seasons. FT% is 0.1096 vs 0.1105. That's why career stays in FG%/FT% and nowhere else.

**One place your instinct does hold.** More history than one season helps. The half-and-half blend beats the board's method by a small but real margin (0.3890 vs 0.3924; the 95% range of the gap, −0.0053 to −0.0015, excludes zero). The gain comes from using the last three seasons, not the full career. This is an option for you to decide on. I haven't built it.

**What the test doesn't cover:** it uses last season's actual minutes, so it checks rates, not minutes calls.

## 3. Are we still following expert projections?

**Not as the source of the numbers.** Here is where the outside sources (Yahoo, Hashtag, RotoBaller, Rotoworld, RotoWire) still come in, per `after-report-2026-10-12-wo5.md` and `pre-draft-workorder-2026-10-01.md`:

1. **Range check.** Any stat outside *every* outside projection either gets pulled back into range or keeps a stated reason. That's now 23 players and 25 stats, down from 131 players and 435 stats before WO-5.
2. **Minutes for long-injury returnees** (Lillard, Haliburton, Kyrie, Trae, Davis, VanVleet, Morant, Dejounte Murray, Edey) come from the outside projections. Our own data can't say how much they'll play.
3. **Top-60 review.** Every name 25+ places off any of five outside rankings gets a one-line explanation. Agreement (rank correlation, where 1.0 means identical order) is 0.828 with Rotoworld, 0.824 with RotoBaller, 0.798 with RotoWire's panel and 0.839 with Yahoo's XRank. All rose a little after WO-5.
4. **Expert notes** (e.g. RotoWire's panel) are treated as leads. They move a line only after two dated outlets confirm them.

Where we disagree with the experts, it's mostly about value, not data. We're higher on players who win steals, free throws and threes on their 2025-26 rates (Daniels, Rollins, Kawhi, Markkanen). We're lower on stars who cost categories (Giannis's free throws, Trae's turnovers).
