## Short answer

1. **You're mostly right.** Career averages play a small part in the board, and they never set a player's production. They enter one place only: FG% and FT% are pulled one-third of the way toward the player's career percentage. Everything else comes from last season (2025-26), or from a three-season average for steals and blocks.
2. **Partly.** Expert projections are used as guardrails on the board's own lines. They don't produce the lines. The board is built from each player's real 2025-26 production, then checked against four outside sources (RotoWorld, RotoWire, RotoBaller, Yahoo). Where the board falls outside every outside projection, it gets corrected or has to carry a stated reason.

All numbers below use **2019-20 to 2025-26 only**, the recent-era test committed on 10/13 after you objected to the 20-year window. I ran no new tests and changed nothing.

## 1. How each line is built

The rules are at the top of `report/wo5/tools/wo5_lines.py:3-9`, and I checked them against the code:

| Category | Source | Career involved? |
|---|---|---|
| Minutes | 2025-26 minutes. Changed only when two dated reports show a new role. | No |
| FGA, FTA, 3PM, REB, AST, TOV | 2025-26 per-36 rate × projected minutes | No |
| STL, BLK | Last three seasons, weighted 5/4/3 toward the recent ones | No |
| **FG%, FT%** | 2025-26 percentage, **moved one-third toward career** | **Yes, at one-third weight** |
| PTS | Worked out from attempts and percentages | Only through the FG%/FT% pull |

If a player played fewer than 25 games in 2025-26, the base drops back to 2024-25, then 2023-24 (`wo5_lines.py:5`). Rookies use a college translation instead.

So if your point is that career numbers aren't really driving the projections, the code agrees. Two-thirds of each shooting percentage is still last season, and every counting stat ignores career completely.

## 2. Why it's built that way: recent-era evidence

On 10/13, five ways of projecting were tested on every player-season from 2019-20 to 2025-26 for players who averaged 20+ minutes the season before (1,528 player-seasons). Source: `scope-fence-2026-10-13/out/modern/modern_history_20mpg.json`. The score is a combined error across all nine categories, and lower is better:

| Method | Error (20+ min) | Error (26+ min) | Beat last-season-only |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Career averages** | **0.4176** (worst) | **0.4255** (worst) | **1 of 7 seasons** |
| Last season only | 0.4060 | 0.4080 | — |
| **The board's method** | 0.3924 | 0.3921 | 7 of 7 |
| Three-season average (5/4/3) | 0.3921 | 0.3926 | 7 of 7 |
| Half last season, half three-season | **0.3890** (best) | **0.3889** (best) | 7 of 7 |

What this shows:
- **Leaning on career averages would make the board worse.** It was the worst method at both minute cutoffs and beat last season alone in just 1 of 7 seasons. Careers include early-development years, and for veterans, a version of the player that no longer exists.
- **The small career pull on percentages does help.** FG% error is 0.2609 on the board against 0.2826 with last season alone; FT% is 0.1096 against 0.1183. Shooting percentages are noisy over one season, and a longer sample steadies them.
- **The half-and-half blend scores slightly better than the board.** The gap is small but real (0.0015–0.0053). This is the open decision from 10/13 (D-SF-4, option c). It is not on the board.

### Your 8-year point applies here too
"Career" in the percentage pull means every season in the data file, and the file starts in 2003-04. I counted the 326 players who get the pull:
- **239 (73%) have 8 or fewer seasons on file.** For most players, "career" really is recent.
- **26 have careers starting before 2013-14.** Their FG%/FT% pull reaches into the older era: LeBron (2003-04), Durant, Horford, Conley (2007-08), Westbrook, Curry, Harden, DeRozan, Holiday, George, Kawhi, Butler, Kyrie, Davis, Lillard and others.

## 3. Expert projections: how they're used

Expert lines don't produce the board, but they check it in four ways (`after-report-2026-10-12-wo5.md`, `pre-draft-workorder-2026-10-01.md` WO-5):

1. **Range check.** Every line is compared with all the outside projections. If two or more counting stats fall outside every expert on the same side, the player's minutes get adjusted. This check cut the cells outside every outside projection from **435 (131 players) to 25 (23 players)**, and each of those 25 kept a stated reason.
2. **Players returning from injury.** Minutes for Lillard, Haliburton, Kyrie, Trae Young, Davis, VanVleet, Morant, Dejounte Murray and Edey come from where the outside projections put them, because our own data can't say how much they'll play.
3. **Rookies and shooting.** A rookie's line, or a shooting cell with no solid 2025-26 base, is moved to the edge of the expert range.
4. **Top-60 review.** The top 60 is checked name by name against the five market files, and every gap of 25+ places has a one-line explanation.

Rank agreement with the experts after the 10/12 rebuild (`wo5/out/rho_market_2026-10-12.json`; 1.0 means identical order): RotoWorld 0.83, RotoWire 0.80, RotoBaller 0.82, Yahoo 0.84. That's close to the consensus without copying it. The remaining gaps are where the board's 9-cat math disagrees with the market.

**What the board does not do:** it doesn't change a player's usage when they join a new team (Brown and LeBron in PHI, Giannis in MIA, Trae and Davis in WAS). You said on 10/12 that new-team usage is essential. That is decision D-SF-4, still open, and the default is no change. The range check only catches cases where a new team pushes a line past every expert.
