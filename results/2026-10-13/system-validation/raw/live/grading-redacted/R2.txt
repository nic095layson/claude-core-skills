# Are career averages in the projections, and are we following expert projections?

**Short answer:** Your objection is mostly right. Career numbers feed only 2 of the 9 categories, FG% and FT%. And no, we are no longer following expert projections. ## 1. What the projections actually do with history

This is from the script that builds every line (`report/wo5/tools/wo5_lines.py`, lines 6–8 and 67–73). I checked it against the script itself, not just against the report that describes it.

| Category | What it is built from | Career used? |
|---|---|---|
| PTS, REB, AST, 3PM, TOV | **2025-26 only**: his per-36 rate × projected minutes | **No** |
| STL, BLK | Three seasons (2025-26 / 24-25 / 23-24, weighted 5/4/3 by games) × minutes | **No** (three seasons, not career) |
| FG%, FT% | 2025-26 rate, moved **one-third of the way toward his career rate** | **Yes** |

Points are calculated from shot volume and percentages, so the career pull on FG% and FT% moves points a little. Nothing else touches career data. If a player didn't play 25+ games in 2025-26, his most recent season with 25+ games is used instead: 23 players use 2024-25 and 1 uses 2023-24.

**So your objection holds for 7 of 9 categories.** Last season decides the volume stats outright. Steals and blocks look back three years, never a full career. If I told you earlier that career averages were "taken into account" without saying it was percentages only, that overstated it.

Towns shows how this plays out. His line is his 2025-26 season: 20.2 pts, 11.9 reb and 1.5 threes in 31.0 minutes. He dropped from #6 to #25 because the board stopped carrying his bigger 2024-25 season. Career averages would have propped him up.

## 2. Should career averages count more? What recent seasons say

The test asked: for every fantasy-relevant player-season from 2019-20 to 2025-26 (20+ minutes and 25+ games the year before), which method would have predicted the season best? The score is the average error, scaled per category; lower is better. The file is `scope-fence-2026-10-13/out/modern/modern_history_20mpg.json`.

| Method | Error | Seasons it beat last-season-only |
|---|---|---|
| **Full career averages** | **0.4176** (worst) | **1 of 7** |
| Last season only | 0.4060 | — |
| **The board's current method** | 0.3924 | 7 of 7 |
| Three-season average | 0.3921 | 7 of 7 |
| Half last season, half three-season | **0.3890** (best) | 7 of 7 |

What this shows:
- **Full career averages are the worst method tested.** That's even true for veterans with 9+ years, where career averages scored 0.4323 and never beat last-season-only (0 of 7). Old seasons from a different role, age or era drag the line in the wrong direction. This fits your point that the game has changed.
- **Career data does earn its place in the percentages.** Pulling FG% toward career beat pulling it toward a three-season average (FG error 0.2609 vs 0.2672; FT 0.1096 vs 0.1105). Shooting is the most stable skill, so a longer record helps there.
- **The best tested method looks back three seasons, not a full career.** Half last season plus half the three-season average beat the board's method. The edge is small but held up when resampled (−0.0053 to −0.0015, which doesn't include zero).
- **Two limits.** Among starters (26+ minutes), the board's method and the plain three-season average are effectively tied (0.3921 vs 0.3926). And the test applies each method at last season's actual minutes, so it doesn't capture the camp role changes or the expert-range check.

**Bottom line:** more career data would make the projections worse. Somewhat more three-season data would likely make them slightly better.

## 3. Are we following expert projections?

**Not as the source of the lines.** Until 10/12, most star lines were older estimates, mostly shaped like 2024-25. WO-5 replaced them with lines built from Basketball-Reference stats. The experts now play five roles:

1. **Range check on every top-150 stat.** Each one is checked against Yahoo, Hashtag and RotoBaller, plus the player's 2025-26 actual. When a line falls outside all of them, fixed rules settle it: a minutes adjustment, keeping it with a stated reason, or moving it to the edge of the range. After WO-5, stats outside every outside line fell from 131 players and 435 stats to 23 players and 25 stats, each kept for a stated reason.
2. **Minutes for players returning from long injuries.** Our own data can't say how much they'll play, so these come from the outside lines: Lillard 27.8, Haliburton 31.3, Kyrie 31.1, Trae Young 32.9, Davis 29.6, VanVleet 31.0, Morant 25.5, Dejounte Murray 29.1, Edey 26.6. Ty Jerome is at 27.6 through the same range rule after his move to starter.
3. **Rookies.** Their lines come from a college-to-NBA translation, and any stat outside the outside lines is moved to the edge of their range.
4. **Ranking comparison.** Our board agrees fairly closely with the outside 9-cat rankings: Rotoworld 0.828, RotoBaller 0.824, RotoWire's expert panel 0.798, Yahoo's own ranking 0.839 (rank correlation, where 1.0 means identical order). The remaining disagreement is intended, because it comes from category math.
5. **Price.** Yahoo ADP is the market column on the card.

Camp role changes need two dated news reports, and they change minutes only, never per-minute rates. Preseason games count as evidence of a player's role, not of his minutes or production. That's your 10/12 rule.
