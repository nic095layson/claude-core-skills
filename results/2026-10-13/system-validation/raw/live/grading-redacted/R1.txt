## 1. Are career averages taken into account?

**Your objection is mostly right.** Career numbers touch only two of the nine categories, and only partly.

| Category | What the board's line uses | Career's role |
|---|---|---|
| PTS, REB, AST, 3PM, TOV (and FGA/FTA) | 2025-26 per-36 rates × projected minutes (`wo5_lines.py:6`) | **None** |
| STL, BLK | Last three seasons weighted 5/4/3 (`:8`) | **None.** This is a three-season window, not career |
| FG%, FT% | 2025-26 percentage, moved **one-third** of the way toward career (`:7`, `:69-70`) | **Partial**, one-third weight |
| PTS (again) | Calculated from shot volume and the percentages (`:9`) | Indirect only, through FG%/FT% |

- **Example:** Towns' line is .508 / .852. That's his 2025-26 shooting moved a third of the way toward his career rates. His points, rebounds and threes are 2025-26 only, which is why he fell from #6 to #25.
- **What "career" means in the code:** every regular season in the Basketball-Reference file, 2003-04 to 2025-26. That covers every active player's full career. LeBron entered in 2003-04, though I didn't check this line by line. It also means a veteran's percentages carry seasons from a different era, which is the issue you raised about the 20-year window. Percentages shift less across eras than three-point volume does, but I haven't measured that.
- **Should career count for more?** The recent-era data says no:
  - In each test, predicting from career averages alone came out worst. Its error score was 0.4176 vs 0.3924 for the board's method, and it beat last-season-only in just 1 of 7 seasons.
  - The best performer was a half-and-half mix of last season and the three-season average (0.3890).
  - The board's one-third pull toward career does improve FG% and FT% over last season alone (FG error 0.261 vs 0.283, FT 0.110 vs 0.118). A three-season percentage did slightly better again (0.260 / 0.104).

**So:** career numbers affect only the two shooting percentages, by design, and the recent data supports leaning on them less, not more. If what bothers you is that the counting stats rest on one season, the data agrees: the three-season mix beat last-season-only in 7 of 7 seasons.

## 2. Are we following expert projections?

**No, and this is deliberate.** The board uses its own method. Expert projections come in at four points:

1. **Range check:** each top-150 line is compared with Yahoo (10/6), Hashtag (10/12), RotoBaller (9/29) and last season's actual numbers. A cell outside all of them gets resolved by fixed rules. That dropped the outside cells from 435 to 25, and each of the 25 is kept with a stated reason.
2. **Players returning from injury:** minutes for Lillard, Haliburton, Kyrie, Trae Young, Davis, VanVleet, Morant, Dejounte Murray and Edey were set to the outside projections, because our own data can't predict how much they'll play.
3. **14 deep bench players** added in August use Hashtag's lines, flagged [ESTIMATED].
4. **Ranking comparison:** the board's ranking tracks Rotoworld at 0.83, RotoBaller at 0.82, RotoWire's experts at 0.80 and Yahoo at 0.84 (1.0 would mean identical order). Matching the experts isn't the goal; the board is meant to disagree where the categories justify it.

**One gap:** the method uses each player's own rates and doesn't model a change in role on a new team, for example Giannis in Miami, Brown and LeBron in Philadelphia, Trae and Davis in Washington. The range check only catches it when a line ends up outside every expert's number.
